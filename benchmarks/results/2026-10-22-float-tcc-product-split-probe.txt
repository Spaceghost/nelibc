TinyCC product-split screening probe
====================================

Question
--------
Can a 16-bit partial-product multiply avoid the 64-bit product temporary in
TinyCC's `big_mul_small` limb loop?

Method
------
Compiled `build/mulsplit_shootout.c` with `/tmp/tcc-install/bin/tcc -O2` and
ran six alternating-order rounds over 65 limbs, 400,000 loop iterations per
variant, multiplying by 1,220,703,125 (`5^13`). Compared:

- baseline: one `uint64_t` product per limb, then low/high extraction;
- duplicate: two direct `uint64_t` expressions for low and high words;
- partial: four 16-bit partial products and 32-bit accumulation.

Elapsed nanoseconds by round (baseline, duplicate, partial):

| Round | Baseline | Duplicate | Partial | Duplicate / baseline | Partial / baseline |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 44,309,656 | 49,832,512 | 155,726,438 | 1.12 | 3.51 |
| 1 | 43,583,582 | 48,435,101 | 152,833,008 | 1.11 | 3.51 |
| 2 | 43,150,009 | 49,356,909 | 153,686,879 | 1.14 | 3.56 |
| 3 | 43,616,374 | 49,091,008 | 153,667,939 | 1.13 | 3.52 |
| 4 | 43,544,467 | 48,919,407 | 151,938,509 | 1.12 | 3.49 |
| 5 | 43,033,216 | 50,599,131 | 158,871,800 | 1.18 | 3.69 |

Result
------
Reject both alternatives. Duplicating the 64-bit expression was 11–18% slower;
the 16-bit partial-product implementation was 3.49–3.69x slower. The output
macro probe also still materialized and spilled the 64-bit product, so output
parameters alone do not remove TinyCC's stack temporary.

This is a screening microbenchmark in C, not a full Nelua parser shootout. No
production source was changed. Probe source and executable are under `build/`.
