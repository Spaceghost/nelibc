TinyCC big_mul_small code generation follow-up
Date: 2026-10-22

After the rolling-pointer optimization, TCC's big_mul_small has a 48-byte stack
frame. Its inner loop no longer computes indexed addresses, but product and
carry are still spilled around the 64-bit multiply, low-word store, and high-word
carry extraction. The helper is called once per multiply-by-5^13 chunk.

A TCC-only candidate inlined the 5^13 chunk loop into big_mul_power5, removing
those repeated calls and helper frames. It passed the float differential corpus
but 12 alternating pairs showed median paired regressions of 0.1–0.7% on all
three long parsers. Short results were also mixed, ranging from a 1.0%
regression to a 0.4% improvement. The candidate is rejected; the rolling-pointer
helper remains. Measurements are in
2026-10-22-float-power5-inline-chunk-rejected-ab.csv.

A second attempt used a union to read the low and high product words directly.
With aggregate initialization, TinyCC generated a memcpy call and long-parser
time regressed 59–65% in paired medians. Direct assignment through a no-init
union removed the memcpy, but long inputs still regressed 0.9–2.4%. Neither
variant is retained; paired results are in
2026-10-22-float-power5-product-union-rejected-ab.csv.

A direct `mull` inline-assembly probe did not improve the TCC code shape. The
current high-level product loop uses a 48-byte frame and spills one 64-bit
product. Returning the assembly's low/high words through C output variables
increased the probe frame to 64 bytes and added more stack loads/stores plus a
saved `rbx`. A second output-to-memory constraint probe was malformed in
TinyCC's lowering and was discarded. No production change or parser timing was
made for these assembly probes.

A direct-site TCC/x86-64 macro used `mull`, added the low-word carry, and
wrote the low and high words straight to the limb and carry storage. The
assembly removed the 64-bit product stack temporary and did not add a helper
call or per-limb register save. It passed the TinyCC float differential corpus;
the guarded fallback source also compiled with Zig and GCC.

The 48-pair full-parser shootout improved short inputs by 0.7–2.0%, but long
inputs regressed by 0.3–1.3%. An instrumented power5-only timing showed a
3.5–7.1% function-level improvement, which did not carry through to complete
long parser calls. Reject the macro for production. Raw end-to-end data:
`2026-10-22-float-power5-mul-asm-macro-ab.csv`; scoped cycle data:
`2026-10-22-float-power5-mul-asm-macro-cycle-ab.csv`.
