Knuth multiply/subtract 32-bit borrow candidate
Date: 2026-10-22

The candidate replaced the current 64-bit subtraction and high-bit borrow
extraction with 32-bit subtraction and explicit borrow comparisons. It passed
the float differential corpus on TinyCC/glibc, Zig/glibc, GCC/glibc, and
Zig/musl.

Twelve alternating baseline/candidate pairs per parser were measured for
standard short inputs and the long-decimal workload. The results in the paired
CSV are mixed: short cases regress up to 3.5% (Zig/musl strtod), while long
cases range from a 3.1% gain (Zig/glibc strtold) to a 2.1% regression (TinyCC
strtod). The candidate is rejected; the existing 64-bit borrow formulation
remains in src/stdlib_float.nelua.
